Local ToolsOur Heritage · Our Strength

Discover six amazing tools from Nepal that our ancestors invented. Simple, smart, and still super useful today!

Kodalo

Uses: A strong digging tool. Farmers use it to turn soil, make holes for seeds, and prepare fields for planting. It has a sharp iron blade and a wooden handle.

Dhiki

Uses: A traditional wooden beater for rice and grains. When you step on one end, the heavy hammer pounds the grain to remove the husk. No electricity needed!

Supa

Uses: A flat bamboo basket used to separate grains from chaff. You shake and toss the basket; the wind blows away the light husks while the heavy grains stay behind.

Namlo

Uses: A strong head-strap made of jute or cloth. It helps carry heavy loads like firewood, water pots, or baskets on your back. The weight rests on your forehead, making it easier to walk uphill.

Theki

Uses: A wooden container used to churn curd into butter and buttermilk. You wind a rope around a stick and pull it back and forth to spin the churner inside. It's a clever way to make fresh butter!

Khurpa

Uses: A small, curved knife used for cutting grass, harvesting crops, and trimming plants. It's lightweight, easy to carry, and perfect for small farm tasks.

ADVANTAGES

Why local tools matter

Made from Natural Materials

These tools use wood, bamboo, iron, and jute — all found in nature. They are eco‑friendly and don't pollute the earth.

Easy to Repair

If a part breaks, you can fix it with simple tools. No need for a mechanic or expensive spare parts!

No Electricity Needed

They work completely by hand or muscle power. Perfect for villages and places where electricity is not always available.

Affordable for Everyone

Local tools are cheap to make and buy. Almost every family can own them without spending a lot of money.

Durable & Long‑Lasting

Built with strong materials and a simple design, these tools can last for many years — sometimes for generations!

Keep Our Culture Alive

Using these tools connects us to our grandparents' wisdom. They remind us of our roots and traditional skills.

DISADVANTAGES

Challenges to consider

Hard Physical Labor

These tools require a lot of muscle power. Using them all day can be tiring and physically demanding.

Time Consuming

Manual work takes longer. Compared to modern machines, finishing tasks can be much slower.

Limited Efficiency

They cannot process large amounts quickly. For big harvests or heavy jobs, they may not be enough.

Weather Dependent

Some tools like the supa need wind to work. Bad weather can stop your work completely.

Requires Skill & Practice

Using them correctly takes training. Without practice, you may hurt yourself or damage the tool.

Not for Large Scale

They are best for small farms and homes. For big industries, they simply cannot compete.
